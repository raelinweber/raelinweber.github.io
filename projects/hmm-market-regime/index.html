01 · Research overview

What we investigated

This project examined whether a Gaussian Hidden Markov Model could infer latent market regimes and filter a 20/50-day simple moving-average crossover and a 20-day breakout strategy. The HMM was used as a regime-based risk filter, not to forecast exact future prices.

02 · Methodology

How the framework worked

  • Historical daily S&P 500 OHLCV data from 2000–2024.
  • Candidate inputs included realised volatility, SMA measures, RSI (14) and Rate of Change (14).
  • Training-only correlation filtering at |r| > 0.80 and training-only standardisation.
  • Diagonal-covariance Gaussian HMMs with 2, 3 and 4 candidate states; BIC selected the model for each rolling training window.
  • Twenty random initialisations per candidate state count, with a documented numerical fallback.
  • Favourable states identified separately for each strategy using training-only positive mean gross open-to-open returns on eligible long signals.
  • 31-observation rolling training window; 2000–2005 pilot rows excluded from final HMM train/test rows.
  • Close(t) signal, open(t+1) execution and open(t+2) holding-period valuation.
  • 5 bps transaction costs plus 2 bps slippage per position change.
The final run used chronological walk-forward predictions, training-window-only scaling and feature selection, and an audited prediction checkpoint.
03 · Results

Out-of-sample performance

The final run generated 4,749 regime predictions from 16 February 2006 to 30 December 2024, and 4,747 next-open holding-period returns from 17 February 2006 to 27 December 2024.

Interpretation: Filtering reduced maximum drawdown for both strategies, but it also reduced net cumulative returns and annualised Sharpe ratios. The results indicate a possible downside-risk benefit, not an improvement in the study's main Sharpe-based performance criterion. A numerical DSR was not reported because the effective independent number of performance-selection trials could not be established.
04 · Limitations & collaboration

What the results do and don't show

  • 31-observation training window; the earlier methodology contains a disclosed 30-versus-31-observation wording inconsistency.
  • Some windows retained only one feature; feature dimensions and hidden-state labels could vary.
  • High posterior probabilities do not prove economic validity of hidden states.
  • The S&P 500 cash index is not directly investable; this is a historical index-price simulation.
  • Transaction costs and slippage are simplified, and findings are limited to one index, one period and two strategies.
